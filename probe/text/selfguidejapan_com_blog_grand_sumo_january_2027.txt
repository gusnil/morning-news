If you are planning a January trip and wondering whether to build your itinerary around it, this guide covers the dates, tickets, seating, etiquette, and how to slot it into a wider self-guided route.
 🏆 Quick Answer: Grand Sumo January 2027
 Dates: January 10–24, 2027 — 15 straight days.
 Venue: Ryogoku Kokugikan, 1-3-28 Yokoami, Sumida-ku, Tokyo — two minutes from JR Ryogoku Station.
 Tickets on sale: December 5, 2026 for general public sale. The New Year tournament frequently sells out on day one.
 Prices: arena (chair) seats from about ¥3,500, realistically ¥5,000–¥10,000; box seats (masu-seki) from around ¥40,000 for four people.
 Best time to arrive: from about 2 p.m. — the top division enters the ring around 3:45 p.m. and the biggest bouts run after 4 p.m.
 When and Where the January Tournament Happens
 The January tournament, the Hatsu Basho , runs January 10 to January 24, 2027 — 15 straight days of matches at Ryogoku Kokugikan, the sport's spiritual home, in Tokyo's Sumida ward just east of the Sumida River. Tickets go on general sale from December 5, 2026 , and pre-orders through some resellers open earlier, in late August of the previous year.
 The venue address is 1-3-28 Yokoami, Sumida-ku, Tokyo . It is a two-minute walk from the West Exit of JR Ryogoku Station on the Sobu Line, and five minutes from Exit A3 of Ryogoku Station on the Toei Oedo Line. From Shinjuku, Shibuya or Tokyo Station you are looking at 20–30 minutes on public transport.
 Ryogoku Kokugikan, two minutes from the station. The green roof and the banner-lined approach are the landmark — you will not walk past it by mistake. The Daily Schedule
 Doors open at 8:45 a.m. on most days, later on the final weekend (10:30 a.m. on the closing Friday, 10:00 a.m. on the closing Sunday). Bouts run until around 6 p.m.
 The excitement builds slowly. Morning bouts feature lower-ranked wrestlers from the bottom four divisions; juryo comes on around 2 p.m., and the top makuuchi division enters the ring at roughly 3:45 p.m. The biggest names and the fullest house come after 4 p.m. If you only have an afternoon, arriving at 2 p.m. gets you the whole build-up.
 How the Sumo Rankings Work
 Sumo runs on a strict hierarchy called the banzuke , republished before every tournament. There are six divisions , from lowest to highest: Jonokuchi, Jonidan, Sandanme, Makushita, Juryo and Makuuchi.
 The critical dividing line sits between Juryo (second division) and Makushita (third) — the boundary between paid professionals and unpaid trainees. Only on promotion to juryo does a wrestler earn a salary and get to wear the formal topknot.
 Within makuuchi, the internal ranks from the top down are yokozuna, ozeki, sekiwake, komusubi , then the numbered maegashira . Yokozuna is the highest rank in the sport and is only awarded to wrestlers who win two major tournaments in a row.
 One practical consequence for ticket-buyers: wrestlers in the top two divisions compete every single day of the tournament, while the bottom four divisions fight on just seven of the fifteen days. Whichever day you pick, you will see makuuchi.
 Who to Watch at Hatsu Basho 2027
 The January banzuke is confirmed in late December 2026 , so treat any earlier prediction as provisional — injuries and the November results move everything. That said, these are the names shaping the top of the sport going into 2027:
 Onosato — reached sumo's highest rank for the May 2025 tournament, a record-fast 13 tournaments after his professional debut, and only the second college-trained wrestler to make yokozuna after Wajima.
 Hoshoryu — promoted to yokozuna in January 2025.
 Aonishiki — Ukrainian-born, of the Ajigawa stable, and the young ozeki charging up behind them after back-to-back titles.
 Two yokozuna on the banzuke plus an ozeki in form is what gives a tournament real bite: when there is more than one yokozuna, the final Sunday tends to deliver a genuinely tense finish.
 Tickets: How to Actually Get In
 Tickets for the January tournament go on general sale in early December — December 5, 2026 for this edition. New Year tickets are the most sought-after of the Tokyo tournaments and frequently sell out on the first day of public sale . Set a calendar reminder, create your account beforehand, and be ready to check out fast.
 Where to Buy
 The Japan Sumo Association's official ticket site (sumo.pia.jp) — the primary route
 Ticket Oosumo (English site)
 Licensed resellers that ship internationally
 The box office at Ryogoku Kokugikan for same-day tickets on quieter weekdays
 Two practical warnings. Online purchases are often collection-only at a Seven-Eleven in Japan — check the confirmation screen and email for the pickup method. And tickets cannot be changed or cancelled once purchased , for any reason. Organizers can also alter schedules at short notice, so check the official site before you travel.

 A Day at the Kokugikan
 The tournament day runs on a rhythm, from the early-morning lower divisions to the top-division entrance in embroidered kesho-mawashi aprons. The moments worth timing your visit around:
 ~2:15 p.m. — the juryo ring-entering ceremony
 ~3:45 p.m. — the makuuchi ring-entering ceremony, the most photogenic moment of the day
 ~4:00–5:55 p.m. — the top-division bouts, when the arena is fullest and loudest
 End of day — the yumitori-shiki , the bow-twirling ceremony that closes proceedings
 One thing that surprises first-timers: the fastest top-division bout can be over in under three seconds. Blink and you miss it.
 The nobori go up outside the Kokugikan for each tournament, one banner per wrestler and stable. They are the clearest sign a basho is running — and they come down the day it ends. 💬 From our Japan travel team

 The Grand Sumo Calendar in Context
 There are six tournaments a year , each 15 days: three in Tokyo (January, May, September), one in Osaka (March), one in Nagoya (July) and one in Fukuoka (November). Our guides to the September Tokyo tournament and the November Kyushu basho cover the other two you can realistically build a trip around.
 The January tournament sets the tone for the year. A wrestler who wins here starts building his case for the next promotion — two consecutive strong tournaments with a title is often what gets an ozeki promoted, and eventually a yokozuna crowned.
 Etiquette and Fun Facts
 Cushion throwing. When a maegashira beats a yokozuna, the crowd hurls the flat arena cushions. It is officially discouraged, and it still happens.
 Grip-locked bouts. When two wrestlers dig in on the mawashi belt, a match runs far past the average — and the hall goes quiet.
 No flash, no tripods, no big luggage. Box seats have room for one cushion per person, so a suitcase simply will not fit.
 The Emperor's Cup is presented to the makuuchi champion on the final day. The closing ceremony is worth staying for.
 Food and Drink
 You can eat inside the venue — bento, yakitori, beer. But the better move is chanko-nabe near the station: the hot pot that sumo stables cook by the vat, served at restaurants around Ryogoku, some of them run by retired wrestlers.
 Winter vs Other Seasons for a Sumo Trip
 January in Tokyo is cold but dry and sunny — good walking weather. You miss the cherry blossoms of late March (see our Tokyo cherry blossom 2027 guide if that is your priority), the June rainy season, the humid July–August months, and the Golden Week crush. If you want sumo plus culture without fighting crowds, Hatsu Basho beats the May and September Tokyo tournaments. Our Japan in January 2027 guide sets the wider month in context.
 Worth knowing: outside tournament windows Ryogoku can feel deserted. The neighbourhood only really comes alive during a basho.
 Building a Real Itinerary Around Hatsu Basho 2027
 🗓️ A 7-Day Frame
 Days 1–2
 Arrive Tokyo. Base near Ueno, Asakusa, or Ryogoku itself.
 Day 3
 Sumo day — a full afternoon session (arrive 2 p.m., stay to the close).
 Day 4
 Sumo Museum inside the Kokugikan, the Edo-Tokyo Museum area, chanko lunch.
 Day 5
 Day trip to Nikko or Kamakura.
 Day 6
 Optional second sumo day for the middle-weekend energy, or Yanaka and Nezu.
 Day 7
 Depart.
 If you can only pick one day, aim for the second Saturday or Sunday — the busiest days, with the most drama and the fullest house. Weekdays in the first week are far easier for last-minute tickets.
 Common Mistakes to Avoid
 Turning up at 4 p.m. with no ticket. The good seats went in December.
 Assuming everything takes a credit card. Bring cash.
 Booking a hotel far out and leaving early — you miss the closing ceremonies.
 Ignoring the lower-ranked wrestlers. Some of the best sumo of the day happens in juryo.
 Forgetting that online tickets cannot be refunded or changed once purchased.

 FAQ
 When exactly is Grand Sumo January 2027? expand_more The Hatsu Basho 2027 runs January 10–24 at Ryogoku Kokugikan in Tokyo — 15 consecutive days.
 When do tickets go on sale? expand_more General public sales open December 5, 2026, through the official Japan Sumo Association ticket portal. The New Year tournament often sells out on the first day.
 How much are tickets? expand_more Arena seats run from about ¥3,500 at the top of the upper deck to around ¥10,000 for lower rows. Box seats for four start around ¥40,000.
 Do I need to speak Japanese? expand_more No. The official site has English, English audio guides are available on site, and the venue has bilingual signage.
 Can I bring food and drink? expand_more Yes in most seat categories. Ringside tamari-seki seats do not allow eating or drinking.
 What if the tournament sells out? expand_more Try same-day tickets at the box office on a weekday, or a licensed reseller. Sumo stable morning practice viewings are another way to see the sport in the same window.
 Is Hatsu Basho better than the other tournaments? expand_more For international visitors, yes: January weather is stable, Tokyo is easy to base in, and the New Year atmosphere adds something you do not get in Nagoya or Fukuoka.
