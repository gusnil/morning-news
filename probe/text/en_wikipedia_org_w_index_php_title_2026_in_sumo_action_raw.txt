*9: The [[Japan Sumo Association]] announces that [[Emperor of Japan|Emperor]] [[Naruhito]] will attend the sumo tournament in Tokyo on 18 January (Day 8). There are applications for a total of 3,469 prize envelopes, which breaks the record set in September of last year. There are also 258 envelopes set to be distributed on 11 January, which will break the previous single-day record set in January of last year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202601100000428.html|title=松嶋菜々子＆斉藤和義も登場、初場所の懸賞申し込みが史上最多3469本　従来を361本上回る|date=10 January 2026|publisher=Nikkan Sports|language=ja|access-date=10 January 2026}}</ref>

Hakunofuji's scheduled opponent, [[Ōnokatsu Kazuhiro|Ōnokatsu]]–who was Nishikifuji's scheduled opponent the day before–becomes the fourth sumo wrestler in history to win two consecutive matches by [[Glossary of sumo terms#fusenshō|default]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sponichi.co.jp/sports/news/2026/01/23/articles/20260123s00005000067000c.html|title=伯乃富士が13日目から休場…足を引きずる様子も　阿武剋は2日連続不戦勝で史上4例目の珍事|date=23 January 2026|publisher=Sports Nippon|access-date=23 January 2026|language=ja}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202601230000750.html|title=伯乃富士は「左母趾MTP側副靱帯損傷」と診断　約４週間の安静加療…13日目から休場|date=23 January 2026|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=23 January 2026|language=ja}}</ref><br>The [[Japan Sumo Association]] announces that the election of directors and deputy directors for the new two-year term at the head of the organization will not be subject to a vote for the fourth consecutive term, as the number of candidates matches the number of positions up for election. It has also been announced that the exact occupancy of the positions will be revealed on 8 March.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/battle/sumo/news/202601230000910.html|title=相撲協会理事候補選は４期連続無投票　春場所後に承認後、八角理事長が続投へ|date=23 January 2026|publisher=Nikkan Sports|access-date=1 February 2026|language=ja}}</ref>
*25: The January 2026 tournament concludes with {{translit|ja|ōzeki}} [[Aonishiki Arata|Aonishiki]] winning the championship [[Glossary of sumo terms#kettei-sen|playoff]] against [[Makuuchi#Maegashira|rank-and-file]] wrestler [[Atamifuji Sakutarō|Atamifuji]], securing his second consecutive [[Yūshō|Emperor's Cup]]. Aonishiki and Atamifuji were tied in the standings entering Day 15 with eleven wins each. After Atamifuji won his scheduled Day 15 contest, which eliminated opponent [[Ōshōumi Seiya|Ōshōumi]] and three others ([[Abi Masatora|Abi]], [[Kirishima Tetsuo|Kirishima]] and [[Ōnosato Daiki|Ōnosato]]) from title contention, Aonishiki defeated {{translit|ja|ōzeki}} [[Kotozakura Masakatsu II|Kotozakura]] to force a playoff–just like he did in the previous tournament last November. The two ({{Win-loss record|w=12|l=3}}) leaders faced each other in the playoff, where Atamifuji pushed Aonishiki to the edge of the ring before Aonishiki stayed low and took his opponent down with a [[Kimarite#Kubinage|headlock throw]]. Aonishiki's victory marks the first time in almost 20 years, since [[Hakuhō Shō|Hakuhō]] in the May 2006 tournament, that a newly promoted {{translit|ja|ōzeki}} has won the championship. He is also the first wrestler in 89 years (since [[Futabayama Sadaji|Futabayama]] in 1937) to win consecutive titles as a newly promoted {{translit|ja|sekiwake}} and a newly promoted {{translit|ja|ōzeki}}. Aonishiki will be a candidate for potential {{translit|ja|yokozuna}} promotion at the next tournament in March. The [[Japan Sumo Association|Sumo Association]] awards runner-up Atamifuji with his third Fighting Spirit prize, one of three [[Sanshō (sumo)|special prizes]] handed out.
