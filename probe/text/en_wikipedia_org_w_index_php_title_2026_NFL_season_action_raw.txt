The '''2026 NFL season''' is the 107th season of the [[National Football League]] (NFL). The regular season began on September 9, 2026, with reigning [[Super Bowl LX|Super Bowl]] champion [[2026 Seattle Seahawks season|Seattle]] defeating [[2026 New England Patriots season|New England]] in the [[NFL Kickoff Game]],<ref name="wed-start"/> and will end on January 10, 2027. The playoffs will begin on January 16 and conclude with [[Super Bowl LXI]] at [[SoFi Stadium]] in [[Inglewood, California]], on February 14.

==Player movement==
The 2026 NFL league year and trading period began on March 11. Beginning March 9, teams were allowed to exercise options for 2026 on players with option clauses in their contracts, submit qualifying offers to their pending restricted free agents, and submit a Minimum Salary Tender to retain exclusive negotiating rights to their players with expiring 2025 contracts and fewer than three accrued seasons of free agent credit. Teams were required to be under the salary cap using the "top 51" definition (in which the 51 highest paid-players on the team's payroll must have a combined salary cap). On March 11, clubs were allowed to contact and begin contract negotiations with players whose contracts had expired and thus became [[unrestricted free agents]].<ref name="calendar">{{cite web|url=https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/nfl-schedule/2025-2026-important-nfl-dates/|title=2025–2026 Important NFL Dates|publisher=National Football League|access-date=October 16, 2025|archive-date=October 13, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251013101430/https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/nfl-schedule/2025-2026-important-nfl-dates/|url-status=live}}</ref>

This season's salary cap was set to $301.2 million per team, a $22 million per team increase from the previous season.<ref>{{cite web|title=NFL salary cap hits milestone at $301.2 million for 2026|url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48055711/nfl-salary-cap-hits-new-milestone-3012-million-2026|website=ESPN.com|date=February 27, 2026|access-date=September 9, 2026|archive-date=July 22, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260722100335/https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48055711/nfl-salary-cap-hits-new-milestone-3012-million-2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Pro Football Hall of Fame members ===
;[[Raymond Berry]]
: Berry played 13 seasons in the NFL as a wide receiver for the [[Baltimore Colts]], and spent over three decades as a coach, including six seasons as the head coach of the [[New England Patriots]]. He was elected to the Hall of Fame in 1973. He was a six-time All-Pro (three first-team, three second-team), six-time Pro Bowler, and two-time NFL champion ([[1958 NFL Championship Game|1958]] and [[1959 NFL Championship Game|1959]]). He died May 25, age 93.<ref>{{Cite web |date=June 1, 2026 |title=Hall of Fame receiver, NFL coach Raymond Berry dies at 93 |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48938330/hall-fame-receiver-nfl-coach-raymond-berry-dies-93 |access-date=June 1, 2026 |website=ESPN.com |archive-date=June 1, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260601142602/https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48938330/hall-fame-receiver-nfl-coach-raymond-berry-dies-93 |url-status=live}}</ref>
;[[Mike Ditka]]
: Ditka played 12 seasons in the NFL as a tight end for the [[Chicago Bears]], [[Philadelphia Eagles]], and [[Dallas Cowboys]] and spent two decades as a coach, including fourteen seasons as the head coach of the Bears and [[New Orleans Saints]]. He was elected to the Hall of Fame in 1988. As a player, he was a five-time All-Pro (two first-team, three second-team), five-time Pro Bowler, and Super Bowl champion ([[Super Bowl VI]]). As a coach, he was a two-time [[AP NFL Coach of the Year]] (1985, 1988) and two-time Super Bowl champion ([[Super Bowl XII]] and [[Super Bowl XX]]), the latter as head coach of the Bears. He died October 9, age 86.<ref>{{Cite web |date=October 9, 2026 |title=Legendary coach, Hall of Fame TE Mike Ditka dies at age 86 |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50140701/legendary-coach-hall-fame-te-mike-ditka-dies-age-86 |access-date=June 1, 2026 |website=ESPN.com}}</ref>
;[[Sonny Jurgensen]]
:Jurgensen played 18 seasons in the NFL as a quarterback for the [[Philadelphia Eagles]] and [[Washington Redskins]] and was elected to the Hall of Fame in 1983. He was a four-time All-Pro (two first-team, two second-team), five-time Pro Bowler, and won the [[1960 NFL Championship]] with the Eagles. He died February 6, age 91.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Alper |first=Josh |date=February 6, 2026 |title=Pro Football Hall of Famer Sonny Jurgensen dies at 91 |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/pro-football-hall-famer-sonny-154326068.html |access-date=February 6, 2026 |work=Yahoo Sports}}</ref>

== Rule changes ==
The following rule changes were adopted at the NFL Owners' Meeting on March 31, 2026:<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-new-playing-rules-bylaws-resolutions-for-2026|title=NFL announces new playing rules, bylaws, and resolutions for 2026|date=March 31, 2026|access-date=March 31, 2026|website=NFL.com|archive-date=March 31, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260331205155/https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-new-playing-rules-bylaws-resolutions-for-2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/nfl/2026/03/31/nfl-kickoff-rule-changes-2026-onside-kicks/89409052007/|title=NFL kickoff rules 2026: League makes changes to onside kick, more|date=March 31, 2026|access-date=March 31, 2026|work=USA Today|archive-date=April 1, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260401043639/https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/nfl/2026/03/31/nfl-kickoff-rule-changes-2026-onside-kicks/89409052007/|url-status=live}}</ref>
* The kickoff rules that were implemented in 2024 were modified for the second consecutive year:
** Onside kicks will now be permitted at any time during the game. Previously, they were restricted to the fourth quarter, and only by the trailing team.
** If a kickoff is moved to the 50-yard line due to one or more penalties, a touchback will place the ball at the receiving team's 20-yard line instead of the 35-yard line.
** The formation rules of the receiving team were adjusted to require five players to have their front foot on the setup line. Previously, six players were required at the setup line.
* An expansion to the Instant Replay/Replay Assist rules:
** League officials can consult with the on-field officials and the Replay Assistant to initiate a review about whether a player should be ejected for a flagrant act even if no penalty flag had been thrown.<ref name="ESPN050826">{{Cite web|last=Seifert|first=Kevin|last2=Kahler|first2=Kalyn|date=May 8, 2026|title=Referees Association, NFL ratify 7-year CBA to avoid work stoppage|work=ESPN|url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48713349/referees-association-nfl-ratify-7-year-cba-avoid-work-stoppage|access-date=June 27, 2026|archive-date=May 9, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260509125024/https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48713349/referees-association-nfl-ratify-7-year-cba-avoid-work-stoppage|url-status=live}}</ref> 
**The head coach is allowed to designate a sideline assistant to handle the duty of throwing the red challenge flag to initiate an [[Replay review in gridiron football|instant replay]] review. Previously, only the head coach was permitted to throw the challenge flag. ==Regular season==
The season is to be played over an 18-week schedule, beginning on September 9. Each of the league's 32 teams plays 17 games, with one [[Bye (sports)|bye week]]. The regular season is scheduled to end on January 10, 2027; all games during the final weekend will be intra-division games, as it has been since {{nfly|2010}}.

Each team will play the other three teams in its own division twice, one game against each of the four teams from a division in its own conference, one game against each of the four teams from a division in the other conference, one game against each of the remaining two teams in its conference that finished in the same position in their respective divisions the previous season (e.g., the team that finished fourth in its division would play all three other teams in its conference that also finished fourth in their divisions), and one game against a team in another division in the other conference that also finished in the same position in their respective division the previous season.<ref name="rnfb"/>

===Flexible scheduling rules===
This is the fourth season of the league's flexible scheduling system that includes ''[[Thursday Night Football]]'', ''[[NBC Sunday Night Football|Sunday Night Football]]'', ''[[Monday Night Football]]'', and cross-flexing (switching) of Sunday afternoon games between [[NFL on CBS|CBS]] and [[NFL on Fox|Fox]].<ref name="awfulannouncing20230513">{{Cite web|url=https://awfulannouncing.com/nfl/nfl-flex-scheduling-rules-gives-cbs-and-fox-more-power-to-protect-games.html|title=NFL flex scheduling rules gives CBS and Fox more power to protect games|work=Awful Announcing|date=May 13, 2023|access-date=May 22, 2023|archive-date=May 15, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230515094259/https://awfulannouncing.com/nfl/nfl-flex-scheduling-rules-gives-cbs-and-fox-more-power-to-protect-games.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=May 12, 2023 |title=NFL may use flexible scheduling to move Sunday afternoon games to Monday night |url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/nfl-may-use-flexible-scheduling-to-move-sunday-afternoon-games-to-monday-night |access-date=February 4, 2024 |work=NBC Sports |archive-date=February 4, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240204154436/https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/nfl-may-use-flexible-scheduling-to-move-sunday-afternoon-games-to-monday-night |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="TNF 2025 flex">{{cite web |title=NFL shortens time needed for Thursday night flex to 21 days |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/44480684/nfl-shortens-needed-thursday-night-flex-21-days |work=ESPN |date=March 31, 2025 |access-date=October 15, 2025 |archive-date=April 8, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250408154117/https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/44480684/nfl-shortens-needed-thursday-night-flex-21-days |url-status=live }}</ref>

Flexible scheduling for ''TNF'' games remain the same as the previous season: only two games can be flexed between weeks 13 and 17,<ref name="TNF 2025 flex2">{{cite web |date=March 31, 2025 |title=NFL shortens time needed for Thursday night flex to 21 days |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/44480684/nfl-shortens-needed-thursday-night-flex-21-days |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250408154117/https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/44480684/nfl-shortens-needed-thursday-night-flex-21-days |archive-date=April 8, 2025 |access-date=April 21, 2025 |work=ESPN}}</ref><ref name="2026 flex rules">{{cite web |title=2026 Flexible Scheduling Procedures and Scheduling for Week 18 |url=https://www.nfl.com/legal/flexible-scheduling-procedures |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230525010142/https://www.nfl.com/schedules/flexible-scheduling-procedures |archive-date=May 25, 2023 |access-date=Sep 4, 2026 |website=NFL.com}}</ref> teams are not required to flex into ''TNF'' more than once, teams are not allowed to play more than two Thursday games on short rest during the season, and teams cannot play multiple away ''TNF'' games during the season without their approval, and the league has a deadline of 21 days to flex any eligible game into TNF.<ref name="TNF 2025 flex2" />

The other flexible scheduling rules remained the same as in 2025. Any ''Monday Night Football'' game will be allowed to be flexed between weeks 12 and 17; the league is required to announce its rescheduling no later than 12 days before the contests. For ''Sunday Night Football'', no more than two games can be flexed between weeks 5 and 10, while any game between weeks 11 to 17 could be flexed; the league will be required to give weeks 5 to 13 ''SNF'' games a 12-day notice, and weeks 14 to 17 a 6-day notice. All Week 18 games will be initially listed as "TBD", with the league announcing its schedule after Week 17 games are completed.<ref name="2026 flex rules" />

Additionally, CBS and Fox will have the ability to protect games from being moved (except for Week 18 contests), either from a change to another network or a change of the time slot. When the initial season schedule was created, the two networks selected a limited number of games involving a specific number of teams from their respective conference. Otherwise, every game can be initially scheduled on any network regardless of conference.<ref>{{Cite web |date=March 26, 2021 |title=While details remain sparse, the NFL's TV packages on CBS and Fox will seemingly look much different in 2023 |url=https://awfulannouncing.com/cbs/while-details-remain-sparse-the-nfls-tv-packages-on-cbs-and-fox-will-seemingly-look-much-different-in-2023.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211024201049/https://awfulannouncing.com/cbs/while-details-remain-sparse-the-nfls-tv-packages-on-cbs-and-fox-will-seemingly-look-much-different-in-2023.html |archive-date=October 24, 2021 |access-date=October 23, 2021 |work=Awful Announcing}}</ref> After the season starts, each network is allowed to protect one game each week from being flexed.<ref name="awfulannouncing202305132">{{Cite web |date=May 13, 2023 |title=NFL flex scheduling rules gives CBS and Fox more power to protect games |url=https://awfulannouncing.com/nfl/nfl-flex-scheduling-rules-gives-cbs-and-fox-more-power-to-protect-games.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230515094259/https://awfulannouncing.com/nfl/nfl-flex-scheduling-rules-gives-cbs-and-fox-more-power-to-protect-games.html |archive-date=May 15, 2023 |access-date=May 22, 2023 |work=Awful Announcing}}</ref>

==Notable events==
===Referee collective bargaining agreement===
The [[collective bargaining agreement]] between the NFL and [[NFL Referees Association]] (NFLRA) was set to expire on May 31, 2026. If no agreement had been reached by that date, the league planned to lockout its regular game officials and then use replacement referees for the first time since the [[2012 NFL referee lockout]]. One issue under dispute was that the referees' union wanted an annual salary increase of 10 percent per year for the next six years, while the league had offered just 6.45 percent. The league also wanted additional performance standards such as increasing the probationary period for new officials from three to five years, and to award more playoff games based on performance instead of seniority.<ref>{{cite web|title=Everything to Know About the NFL's Labor Dispute With Refs As CBA Expiration Nears |url=https://www.si.com/nfl/nfl-labor-dispute-with-refs-explained |first=Brigid |last=Kennedy |date=March 31, 2026 |access-date=April 1, 2026 |website=SI.com}}</ref> On May 8, 2026, the NFL and the NFLRA ratified a new 7-year CBA, which runs until the 2032 season.<ref name="ESPN050826"/>{{Clarify|reason=what are the agreement terms?|date=September 2026}}

===Pro Bowl Honors===
On August 26, the NFL announced that the league will no longer hold any future [[Pro Bowl]] games, ending the annual event after 88 years and becoming the first of the [[Major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada|"Big Four" professional sports leagues]] to end their all-star event. Instead, a Pro Bowl team consisting of 88 players will be selected each season under the same format, and all players selected to the team will be invited to a ceremony after the season to be honored.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Jones |first=Jonathan |date=2026-08-26 |title=NFL ending Pro Bowl game. Player honors will remain, but alternates no longer acknowledged |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7540770/2026/08/26/nfl-pro-bowl-eliminated-ending/ |access-date=2026-08-26 |work=The New York Times |issn=0362-4331 |archive-date=August 27, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260827093841/https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7540770/2026/08/26/nfl-pro-bowl-eliminated-ending/ |url-status=live }}</ref>
